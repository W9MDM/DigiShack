# MT63

MT63 is the mode the emergency nets use. ARES and the NBEMS nets that run fldigi and flmsg
send their traffic in MT63-1000L or MT63-2000L, because it copies through noise, fading and
the audio of an FM handheld held up to a speaker, where the narrow modes give up.

It is 64 tones at once, each one slowly changing phase (differential BPSK at 5, 10 or 20
baud). Every character is spread over all 64 tones and, with the long interleave, over 6.4
seconds, under an error-correcting code that still reads it with a third of its bits lost.
A burst of static or a deep fade takes a little from many characters instead of all of one.
It is wide and slow to start, and it is very hard to knock off the air.

The modem is a port of Pawel Jalocha SP9VRC's MT63 as fldigi 4.2.13 carries it
(`src/mt63`). What goes on the air is bit for bit fldigi's, so a DigiShack station and an
fldigi station read each other.

## On the decodes page

Choose **MT63** in the mode list. Choosing it decodes where the dial already is (the list
marks it "here"), as nets announce their own frequencies, and it stays chosen while the dial
stays within 5 kHz. The panel has the mode picker (MT63-1000L to start; 2000L is the common
NBEMS choice on a wide channel) and the ± buttons; the frequency is fldigi's cursor, and the
waterfall marks it and the edges of the 64 carriers. A click on the waterfall moves it.

Text prints some seconds after it is sent: about 10 s at MT63-1000L and 5 s at 2000L. That is
the long interleave, which is what lets MT63 copy through fades, and fldigi is the same. A
few odd characters often print just before an over starts: the transmitter fills the
interleaver with random bits so the receiver can lock, fldigi's does too, and fldigi prints
them the same way.

The exchange bar, F1-F8 and **Log contact** work as in the other keyboard modes; MT63 logs as
ADIF MODE=MT63. RSID both ways, with fldigi's code for each of the six.

## The modes

| Mode | Width | Tone spacing | Characters a second | Interleave | Text appears after |
|------|------:|-------------:|--------------------:|-----------:|-------------------:|
| MT63-500S | 500 Hz | 7.8125 Hz | 5 | 6.4 s | about 13 s |
| MT63-500L | 500 Hz | 7.8125 Hz | 5 | 12.8 s | about 19 s |
| MT63-1000S | 1000 Hz | 15.625 Hz | 10 | 3.2 s | about 6.5 s |
| **MT63-1000L** | 1000 Hz | 15.625 Hz | 10 | 6.4 s | about 10 s |
| MT63-2000S | 2000 Hz | 31.25 Hz | 20 | 1.6 s | about 3 s |
| **MT63-2000L** | 2000 Hz | 31.25 Hz | 20 | 3.2 s | about 5 s |

**S** and **L** are the short and long interleave. Long survives longer fades. Both ends
must use the same mode, interleave included: an L signal does not read as S.

The delay is real and is the price of the interleave. A character is not complete until
all its pieces have arrived, and the receiver then waits a little longer to be sure of the
timing. Expect the text to start several seconds after the signal does. Expect a few junk
characters right before it and right after the end of a transmission, as in fldigi.

## Where it sits

fldigi places MT63 by its **cursor**, the waterfall frequency the operator clicks. The lowest
of the 64 tones is half the width below the cursor and the highest is one tone spacing short
of half the width above it. With the cursor at 1500 Hz:

| Mode | Lowest tone | Highest tone |
|------|------------:|-------------:|
| MT63-500 | 1250 Hz | 1742.2 Hz |
| MT63-1000 | 1000 Hz | 1984.4 Hz |
| MT63-2000 | 500 Hz | 2468.75 Hz |

fldigi has two fixed placements, and the NBEMS nets mostly use the second:

- **Lowest tone at 500 Hz** puts the cursor at 500 + half the width (1000 Hz for MT63-1000).
- **Centred at 1500 Hz** (the SHARES requirement) puts the cursor at 1500 Hz.

With an upper-sideband dial at D MHz and the cursor at 1500 Hz, MT63-2000 covers
D + 500 Hz to D + 2469 Hz. That is why MT63-2000 fits an FM voice channel and why HF nets
prefer MT63-1000: 2 kHz is most of an SSB passband.

## Listening

**Tuning.** Put the cursor where the net puts it, usually 1500 Hz. The receiver searches eight
tones either way, about 62 Hz at MT63-500, 125 Hz at -1000 and 250 Hz at -2000. It finds the
signal to a fraction of a tone and reports how far off it was. A station 10 to 20 Hz off
reads without a touch.

**What it can copy.** On generated signals with white noise (check:mt63), measuring SNR in
2.5 kHz as for the other modes:

- MT63-1000L reads down to about -8 dB (-4 dB in its own 1000 Hz).
- MT63-2000L reads down to about -4 dB.
- MT63-500L reads at -9 dB.

**The squelch.** Each character comes with a signal-to-noise figure from the error
correction. Noise alone reads about 3 and never above 3.2. Text is printed above 3.6, so a
quiet band prints nothing: sixty seconds of noise printed no characters. fldigi shows the
same figure as "s/n" in dB (3.6 is about 5.5 dB).

**Long integration** (fldigi's "long receive integration") averages over 32 symbols instead
of 16. It helps a weak, steady signal and makes the text later still.

## Sending

The transmitter sends what fldigi sends:

1. A preamble the length of the interleave, so the far end is synced before the first
   character.
2. The text.
3. As much again to flush the interleave, so the far end prints the last characters.
4. A final jamming symbol that tells the far end's receiver the signal has gone.

An over is therefore at least twice the interleave long, 13 s in MT63-1000L, plus the text at
10 characters a second.

Accented letters go as fldigi's 8-bit default sends them and come back whole. fldigi's tuning
tones before the MT63 (on by default there, 4 s) are available but off by default; the
receiver does not need them.
